Excellent question. You're distinguishing workflow scheduling from job scheduling, which is an important architectural decision.
Interviewer answer
For this exercise:
- No workflow priority. All workflows have equal priority.
- No strict FIFO execution between workflows. Workflow 2 does not need to wait until Workflow 1 finishes.
- Maximum 4 concurrently executing jobs globally, shared across all workflows.
- Work-conserving scheduling: If execution capacity is available and an eligible job exists in any workflow, the executor should be able to schedule it.
- No strict fairness guarantee between workflows for now.

Yes. We need to track both job-level and workflow-level status. Your reasoning is correct because they answer different questions.
- Job status: What happened to this particular job?
- Workflow status: What is the overall outcome of this workflow execution?
